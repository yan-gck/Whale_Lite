Section A. Directions. In this section, you will hear three news reports. At the end of each news report, you will hear two or three questions. Both the news report and the questions will be spoken only once. After you hear a question, you must choose the best answer from the four choices marked A, B, C and D.

News report one. A city in the north of the country has started a new program that turns old bicycles into public library bikes. Volunteers collect broken bicycles from recycling centers and repair them with parts donated by local shops. Each repaired bike carries a small metal box filled with books. Residents can borrow a book for free and return it at any of the twelve stations across the city. The program began in March and has already repaired more than four hundred bicycles. City officials say they hope the program will encourage people to read while they travel, and reduce the number of bicycles that end up in landfills. Question one. What do volunteers do with the broken bicycles? Question two. What is one aim of the program?

News report two. A study published this week suggests that office workers who take a short walk after lunch are more productive in the afternoon than those who stay at their desks. Researchers followed two hundred employees at a software company for six months. The group that walked for fifteen minutes reported fewer headaches and made fewer mistakes in the afternoon. The researchers warn that the result may not apply to workers who do heavy physical labor. Question three. What did the study find about workers who walked after lunch? Question four. What limitation of the study is mentioned?

News report three. A heavy snowstorm has forced the airport of a northern city to cancel more than ninety flights. The storm began on Thursday evening and continued into Friday morning. Airport staff cleared the main runway three times during the night, but strong winds covered it again with snow. Passengers were given hotel rooms near the airport, and the airport said that normal service should return by Saturday afternoon. Question five. What happened at the airport? Question six. When is normal service expected to return?

Section B. Directions. In this section, you will hear two long conversations. At the end of each conversation, you will hear four questions. Both the conversation and the questions will be spoken only once.

Conversation one. Good morning, I would like to open a bank account, please. Certainly, are you a student at the university? Yes, I am in my second year. Then you can open a student account. It has no monthly fee, and you get a free travel card. That sounds good. What do I need to bring? You will need your student card and a letter from the university confirming your address. I have my student card here, but I did not know about the letter. Can I bring it tomorrow? Of course. The bank is open from nine until five on weekdays, and until noon on Saturday. Is there a minimum amount I have to keep in the account? No, there is no minimum, but if you keep more than five hundred pounds in the account, you will receive a small amount of interest each year. And one more thing, can I use the account to receive money from abroad? Yes, but there is a charge of eight pounds for each transfer from outside the country. Question one. Why can the woman open a student account? Question two. What document does the woman still need to bring? Question three. What happens if she keeps more than five hundred pounds in the account? Question four. How much is charged for a transfer from abroad?

Conversation two. Excuse me, I am looking for the new sports centre. Am I going the right way? Yes, keep going past the library and turn left at the roundabout. The sports centre is the building with the glass front. Thank you. Do you know if it is open to the public today? It is, but the swimming pool is closed until four o'clock because the students have a competition this morning. The gym and the courts are open all day. I mainly wanted to swim. Is the pool usually busy in the evening? Quite busy between six and eight, but it is usually quiet after eight. Also, if you buy a card for ten visits, you get two visits free. That is worth knowing. Do I need to book a court in advance? Yes, for the tennis courts you should book at least a day ahead, either at the desk or on the website. Question five. What is the woman trying to find? Question six. Why is the swimming pool closed this morning? Question seven. When is the pool usually quiet? Question eight. How should the woman book a tennis court?

Section C. Directions. In this section, you will hear three passages. At the end of each passage, you will hear three or four questions. Both the passage and the questions will be spoken only once.

Passage one. Sleep is not simply a period when the body switches off. During the night the brain passes through several stages, and each stage seems to do a different job. In deep sleep the body repairs muscles and strengthens the immune system. In the stage known as rapid eye movement sleep, the brain is almost as active as when we are awake. This is when most dreaming happens, and it is also when new memories are sorted and stored. Students who sleep well after studying remember more of what they have learned than students who stay up late. Unfortunately, many people treat sleep as something they can borrow against. They sleep less during the week and try to catch up at the weekend. Research suggests that this pattern does not fully repair the damage, because the brain cannot store the missing stages in a single long night. Question one. What happens during deep sleep? Question two. What is special about rapid eye movement sleep? Question three. Why does catching up on sleep at the weekend not fully work?

Passage two. Every year millions of people visit national parks, and every year park managers face the same problem, how to let people enjoy a place without damaging it. One solution that has become popular is the shuttle bus. In several parks, visitors now leave their cars in a large car park outside the park and travel inside by bus. The buses run every ten minutes, and visitors can get off wherever they like. The system has reduced traffic inside the parks by more than half, and the air is noticeably cleaner. Not everyone is happy. Some visitors say the buses are crowded in summer, and that they cannot reach the quiet corners they used to drive to. Park managers reply that a little inconvenience for visitors is a small price to pay for protecting the landscape. Question four. What problem do park managers face? Question five. What has the shuttle bus system achieved? Question six. What complaint do some visitors make?

Passage three. The first public libraries in many countries were built with money given by a single wealthy donor. Andrew Carnegie, a Scottish American businessman, paid for more than two thousand five hundred libraries around the world between the 1880s and the 1920s. He did not simply send money. He asked each town to provide the land and to promise to pay for the books and the staff from local taxes. In this way, he argued, the library would belong to the town rather than to him, and it would survive after his gift was spent. Many of those buildings are still in use today, and some have been enlarged several times. Historians say that the conditions he attached were as important as the money, because they taught local governments to treat libraries as a permanent public service rather than a temporary charity. Question seven. What did Carnegie require towns to do? Question eight. Why does the speaker say the conditions were important?
